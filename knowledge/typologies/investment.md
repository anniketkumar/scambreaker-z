# Investment scam playbook

## How it works
Victims are drawn in through social media, messaging apps or dating apps and shown a trading platform with fake profits. They are encouraged to "top up" repeatedly, often in cryptocurrency, and are told to pay fees or taxes before they can withdraw. Investment scams caused the largest losses of any scam type in Singapore in the first half of 2026. Source: Singapore Police Force Mid-Year Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2026.

## Indicators
A series of payments to the same new payee that grow each time (escalation), crypto or cross-border transfers, and payments well above the customer's usual amounts. Coercion signals are usually weak: the victim believes they are investing.

## What to tell the customer
Guaranteed high returns are almost always a scam. Check whether the platform is licensed by the regulator, and be suspicious if you must pay before you can withdraw.

## Recommended response
NUDGE on the first unusual payment, HOLD when escalation continues, and call the customer before releasing further payments.
