# Government official impersonation scam playbook

## How it works
A caller claims to be from a bank, the police or a government agency and says the victim's account is linked to a crime. The victim is kept on the line, isolated and frightened, then told to move savings to a "safe account" for investigation. Losses per case are high and elderly victims are disproportionately affected. Source: Singapore Police Force scam advisories.

## Indicators
The customer is on a call, often from an unknown or spoofed number, while making the payment. The payee was added minutes earlier. The amount is many times the customer's usual payment and may empty a savings account. Sessions are long and hesitant; there may be failed attempts as the caller coaches the victim. A remote-access app may be active.

## What to tell the customer
Government officers and the police will never ask anyone to transfer money to a "safe account" or to stay on the phone while paying. Hang up and call back on an official number. Do not share one-time passwords.

## Recommended response
BLOCK or HOLD. Call the customer on the registered number only, alert the trusted contact, and escalate to the police anti-scam centre if the customer insists on paying.
