# Singapore Protection from Scams Act 2025 (summary)

## What the Act allows
The Police may issue a restriction order (RO) to banks to restrict the banking transactions of a person believed to be about to transfer money to scammers. Restrictions cover transfers out of the person's accounts (online and mobile banking, PayNow and over the counter), ATM facilities and credit facilities. An RO is a last resort, issued after other attempts to convince the person have failed. Each order lasts up to 30 days and can be extended up to five times. The person can apply to the Police for withdrawals, for example for daily expenses. The framework came into force on 1 July 2025. Source: Ministry of Home Affairs.

## How often it has been used
As of 15 February 2026, 12 restriction orders had been issued. Graduated restrictions (limits instead of a full freeze) were not prioritised because banks assessed that significant system changes would be required. Source: MHA parliamentary reply, 4 March 2026.

## Relevance to in-transaction scam interception
A full restriction order is blunt and slow. Graduated friction applied inside the payment path (nudge, 24-hour hold, block with a call-back) can stop most scam payments before an order is ever needed, while keeping legitimate payments flowing.
