# Singapore Shared Responsibility Framework (summary)

## Scope
The Shared Responsibility Framework (SRF) from MAS and IMDA took effect on 16 December 2024. It assigns duties to financial institutions and telcos to protect customers from phishing scams, and sets out how losses from unauthorised transactions are shared when those duties are breached. Source: Monetary Authority of Singapore.

## Bank duties under the SRF
Banks must apply a 12-hour cooling period when a digital security token is activated on a new device, send notifications for token activation and outgoing transactions, run real-time fraud surveillance, and provide 24/7 reporting channels with a self-service way to block mobile and online banking access.

## What the SRF does not cover
The SRF targets phishing scams that lead to unauthorised transactions. It does not cover scams where the victim is manipulated into authorising the payment themself, such as investment, job or impersonation scams. For these authorised-push-payment scams, the main protection is detecting the manipulation before the money leaves.
